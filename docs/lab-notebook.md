## What has actually worked before

Pulled from the verified record, not from memory — his ranks skew optimistic when recalled.

| Episode | Result | Percentile |
|---|---|---|
| **S5E12 — Diabetes** | **205 / 4206** | **top 5%** |
| S5E8 — Bank | 1025 / 3365 | top 30% |
| S6E5 — F1 Pit Stops | 1015 / 3022 | top 34% |
| S5E11 — Loan Payback | 1625 / 3724 | top 44% |

S5E12 is the outlier by a wide margin, and it is the only one whose pipeline is written down. What made it different was **not the model** — it was two things:

1. **Feature engineering carried the score.** Digit-level decomposition of every numeric column (ones/tens/hundreds), rounded variants at 0 and 1 dp, numeric pair interactions, digit×numeric combos, digit-combo categoricals, categorical pair crosses — then target and count encoding over *both* the base and the digit-derived categories. Synthetic Playground data is generated from a real table, and the generator leaves fingerprints in the digits.
2. **The blend weights were fitted, not averaged.** LightGBM + a dense Keras net, 5-fold stratified CV across 5 seeds (42/101/202/303/404), and per-seed weights solved by scipy `differential_evolution` (200 iters) directly maximising out-of-fold AUC. Final blend landed near 0.7 LGBM / 0.3 DNN.

The three top-30%-and-worse finishes have no such write-up. Treat that as the lesson: the difference between top 5% and top 34% was a documented, reproducible pipeline.

## The data

13 features, all clean. Three with real cardinality — `Annual_Income_USD` (13,214 uniq), `Daily_Commute_km` (805), `Age` (45) — and everything else is 2–20 levels: `Number_of_Cars_Owned`, two charging-station counts, `Environmental_Concern_Level`, plus six string categoricals (`Gender`, `City_Type`, `Current_Car_Type`, `Home_Charging_Possible`, `Subsidy_Available`, `Range_Anxiety_Level`).

⚠ **pandas 3 reports string columns as `str`, not `object`** — the usual `dtype == "object"` categorical detection silently finds zero of them. And the target ships as `Yes`/`No` strings, not 0/1.

## What separates the top of the board (worked out 2026-09-01 evening)

- **Income is a categorical in disguise.** 13,214 distinct values in train; 98% of *rows* carry an income that occurs in the 10k-row original (54% of the distinct values do — the common values are the original's). Each value is a generator seed with its own target rate, and an 18-leaf tree with `min_data_in_leaf 152` cannot carve 13k levels. A per-value target rate hands it that directly: count encoding is worth +0.0011 (run 4), the original lookup +0.0003 (run 6), and nested target encoding another +0.0020 on top of both (run 8) — **+0.0033 cumulative over the tuned model**. **This is the S5E12 target-encoding step transferring** — the digit factory does not, the encoding does.
- **The original is a weak prior, not a key.** Joining train to the original on income (income is unique in the original for 507k train rows) recovers the original row's other columns at chance level — except `City_Type` (+13 pts over chance) and the label (+1 pt). The synthetic rows are not perturbed copies of original rows, so there is no row-level leak to find. Lookup of the original's per-value label rate is worth a modest +0.0003 (run 6).
- **The board is noisier than it looks.** Bootstrapping OOF predictions onto random 20%-of-test slices (57k rows, the usual public split): **sd 0.00101 for run 6, 0.00089 for run 8 — a 95% band of ± 0.002.** Ranks 2–12 sit within 0.0001 of each other, which is *tighter* than independent models could manage — they are forks of one notebook, not 12 independent results. Do not read rank differences inside ±0.002 as results. (`diag_generator.py`; the public notebook *"Why everyone is stuck at 0.941 on this LB"* reaches the same conclusion with DeLong's closed form.)
- **Dead ends, measured:** `id` order carries nothing (AUC 0.5000, flat by 50k-row block). An 11-feature combo key of everything but income and commute scores 0.59 alone with 30% test coverage — nothing. Ordinal `Range_Anxiety_Level` +0.00002 — noise.
- **Nested TE is not optional.** Fit rows get their encoding from an inner 5-fold OOF, validation rows from the full fit set. Skip the nesting and the OOF lies upward — and CV stops tracking the board, which is the one thing this project has going for it.

## What the generator artifacts actually are

Checked before porting the S5E12 digit factory, and the answer was **don't**:

- **9.21% of incomes sit exactly at the 30000 floor**, with a positive rate of **4.43% against 17.46% overall**. A large mass point with a very different target rate.
- **21.6% of commutes sit exactly at the 5.0 floor** — that pile alone explains the 29% zero-decimal spike.
- Every digit AUC above noise is those two piles leaking through: income's ones-digit is 0 for the whole 30000 pile. Age ones/tens digits are 0.5034 / 0.4942 and the commute decimal is 0.5021 — noise.

So the artifact is two clipped floors, not a digit fingerprint. Run 2 then showed **even the explicit flags don't help** — with 13 features and 668k rows the trees find `income <= 30000` on their own.

**The lesson against S5E12, revised the same evening: the digit factory does not transfer, the encoding step does.** S5E12 had a wide frame the digit factory could exploit; this one is narrow and clean, and hand-built features lose (run 2). But income is a 13k-level generator seed, and target-encoding it — the *other* half of the S5E12 feature stage — is worth +0.0020 on its own (run 8). Hyperparameters were tuned in run 3 and bought +0.0005; that was never the lever.

## Traps from last time

- **Pseudo-labelling leaks through the soft labels — measure it two-stage or not at all.** Runs 14 and 31–34 took their soft test labels from a fold-averaged submission; each fold model had seen the other folds' validation rows, so every fold's training set carried a whisper of its own validation labels. OOF gains of +0.0002 to +0.0005 that were really +0.00004 (run 37). The board said so first — run 14's LB gain was exactly zero. `v22_pseudo_clean.py` is the correct form: fold b's soft labels come only from a model fitted on fold b's fit rows. This is the same class of mistake as un-nested TE, one level up.
- **A full-data refit calibrated on a leaky run's iteration count over-trains.** Run 32 stopped at ~4400 rounds because its inflated validation kept improving; the clean stage-2 stops at ~2000. Take the round count from the leak-free run.
- **`from features import *` skips `_fit`** and every other underscore name; v16 crashed on it after a full data load. Import them by name.
- **Check the top public notebooks' CV before grinding.** Between 09-06 and 09-12 the project did nothing and fell from 170 to 369 because the recipe that beat our whole blend was public for five days. First thing every session: `kaggle kernels list --competition ... --sort-by voteCount` and `--sort-by dateRun`, read the CV numbers in the titles, pull anything above ours. One notebook was worth more than 22 of our own runs.
- **Nelder-Mead from equal weights stalls past ~10 blend groups.** blend_v5's first fit came out *below* blend_v4 with a superset of its groups (0.946346 vs 0.946351) — the optimizer, not the data. `assemble.py` now does a coordinate search over the simplex (each weight on a grid, others held, four passes) and polishes with Nelder-Mead; that found 0.946381.
- **pytabkit's RealMLP ignores MPS on this Mac** — Lightning reports "GPU available: True (mps), used: False" and trains on CPU, ~2 s per epoch per 10k rows.
